There's a moment in every project where I stop thinking about grids, type, and hierarchy — and I just sit with a colour.

It sounds abstract, but it's the most grounded thing I do. Colour isn't decoration. It's the first word in a visual language. Get it right and the rest follows almost naturally. Get it wrong and no amount of clever layout will save you.

My process starts with a single anchor: one hue that carries the emotional weight of the whole piece. Everything else — the supporting palette, the gradient transitions, the dark-mode inversions — is built in relation to that anchor.

The electric violet in PriHues isn't a branding accident. It's deliberate: confident without being aggressive, creative without being chaotic. The cobalt keeps it grounded. The fuchsia gives it a pulse.

That's the language. Learn it once, speak it everywhere.
